Slow-release fertilizer granules, made to order

Granules that feed the crop over weeks instead of days, for fewer applications and less nutrient loss. Formulated to your nutrient content and release period.

A slow-release fertilizer releases its nutrients gradually over weeks or months instead of dissolving at once, which reduces leaching and volatilisation losses and the number of applications. Gujarat Bio Organics makes slow-release granular products to order for bulk and private-label buyers.

How slow release works

Fertilizer that dissolves at once can be lost before the crop uses it: nitrogen leaches or escapes as gas, and phosphorus gets locked in the soil. Slow-release products spread the supply over time.

Slow-release, controlled-release and stabilised

  • Slow-release products release nutrients gradually, but the rate depends on soil moisture, temperature and microbial activity.
  • Controlled-release products, usually polymer-coated, release at a rate set by the coating, mainly driven by temperature.
  • Stabilised products, such as neem-coated urea, slow down the conversion of nitrogen in the soil rather than coating it fully.

Questions buyers ask

What is a slow-release fertilizer?

A fertilizer that releases nutrients gradually over weeks or months, instead of dissolving all at once.

What is the difference between slow-release and controlled-release fertilizer?

Slow-release products release gradually at a rate that depends on soil conditions. Controlled-release products, usually polymer-coated, release at a rate set by the coating.

What are examples of slow-release fertilizers?

Coated and organic-matrix granules, low-solubility nitrogen compounds and organic fertilizers. Neem-coated urea is a stabilised product that slows nitrogen conversion.

Slow-release or water-soluble fertilizer: which should I use?

Water-soluble fertilizer feeds a crop quickly at a precise stage through drip or foliar spray. Slow-release fertilizer gives a steady supply from one basal application. Many programmes use both.

Is neem-coated urea a slow-release fertilizer?

It is usually described as a slow-release or stabilised nitrogen fertilizer, because the neem coating slows the conversion of urea nitrogen in the soil.
