Humic acid, fulvic acid and potassium humate

Humic substances are the stable, dark fraction of well-decomposed organic matter. Commercial humic acid is usually extracted from humified sources such as leonardite or lignite by an alkaline process, and potassium humate is its water-soluble potassium salt. Fulvic acids are the smaller, lighter fraction that stays dissolved across a wide pH range, which is why fulvic-rich grades suit foliar and drip use.

Humic acid or fulvic acid?

Both come from the same humified material. Humic acids are larger molecules that are not soluble in acid, so they work mainly in the soil. Fulvic acids are smaller and soluble at every pH, so they move more easily and suit foliar and drip use. Many products combine both. Read our humic vs fulvic guide.

Potassium humate or humic acid?

Potassium humate is humic acid that has been neutralised with potassium, which makes it water-soluble. Choose potassium humate for drip, drench and liquid formulation, and humic granules for basal application. See potassium humate vs humic acid.

Questions buyers ask

What is humic acid used for in agriculture?

Humic acid is used to improve soil structure, hold nutrients near the root zone, complex micronutrients and support root growth, so crops make better use of the fertiliser already applied. It is most useful in compact, over-cropped or low organic-matter soils.

What is the difference between humic acid and fulvic acid?

Both are humic substances. Humic acid molecules are larger and work mainly in the soil. Fulvic acid molecules are smaller and dissolve at any pH, so they suit foliar and drip use.

Is potassium humate the same as humic acid?

Potassium humate is humic acid neutralised with potassium, which makes it soluble in water. It is used where humic acid has to be dissolved, such as drip, drench and liquid products.

Humic acid granules or liquid: which is better?

Granules suit basal application with fertiliser and broadcast. Liquids and soluble powders suit drip, drench and fertigation. Many growers use granules at sowing and a liquid later in the crop.

Can I mix humic acid with NPK fertiliser, urea or micronutrients?

Humic acid mixes with most fertilisers, including NPK, urea and micronutrients. Avoid mixing with strongly acidic products or concentrated calcium solutions without a jar test, because humic acid can settle out at low pH.

What is the dose of humic acid per acre?

Dose depends on the grade and concentration, so a 1.5% granule and a 51% granule are used at very different rates. Follow the label, or ask us for the crop-wise dose chart for the grade you buy.

What does 'humic acid 98' mean?

It usually refers to high-solubility potassium humate flakes or powder sold on a total-content basis. Our listed grade for sale in India is potassium humate 49% powder. For export, send us the specification you need and we will quote.
