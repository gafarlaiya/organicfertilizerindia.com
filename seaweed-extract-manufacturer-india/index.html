Ascophyllum, Kappaphycus, Sargassum and Spirulina

Seaweed extracts are made by breaking down the cell walls of marine algae and concentrating the soluble fraction. They carry naturally occurring polysaccharides such as alginates, mannitol, betaines, trace minerals and plant hormone-like compounds. Composition depends on the species: Ascophyllum nodosum is a cold-water brown alga, Kappaphycus alvarezii is a red alga farmed in warm coastal waters including India's south-east coast, and Sargassum is a tropical brown alga.

Which seaweed should you choose?

Ascophyllum nodosum is the most widely used and studied seaweed for biostimulants worldwide. Kappaphycus alvarezii is a red seaweed farmed in India, and its extract (often called K-sap) is used widely in Indian field crops. Sargassum is a tropical brown seaweed found along Indian coasts. Blends combine profiles. Read Kappaphycus vs Ascophyllum vs Sargassum.

Liquid or granules?

Liquid extracts suit foliar spray and drip. Seaweed granules carry the extract into the soil with basal fertiliser, which is why they are popular with farmers who broadcast. See our granules range.

Questions buyers ask

What is seaweed extract used for in agriculture?

Seaweed extract is used to help crops tolerate heat, drought and salinity, to support flowering and fruit set, and to support root and shoot growth and recovery after stress. It is applied as a foliar spray, through drip or as granules in the soil.

What is the difference between Ascophyllum, Kappaphycus and Sargassum extracts?

They come from different seaweeds. Ascophyllum nodosum is a North Atlantic brown alga, Kappaphycus alvarezii is a red alga farmed in tropical waters including India, and Sargassum is a tropical brown alga. Each has a different composition, so the best choice depends on crop and purpose.

What does 'Kappaphycus alvarezii 24 (liquid)' mean?

It is the grade name as listed under Schedule VI of the Fertiliser Control Order: Kappaphycus alvarezii seaweed extract at 24% in liquid form. We also make the 9.5% and 7.2% Kappaphycus grades.

Seaweed granules or liquid seaweed: which should I use?

Use granules for basal or broadcast application with fertiliser, and liquid for foliar sprays and drip. Many programmes use granules at sowing and liquid sprays at flowering.

Can seaweed extract be mixed with humic acid?

Yes. Seaweed and humic acid are commonly applied together, and we make ready blends of humic acid plus seaweed extract in liquid, powder and granules.

What is the dose of seaweed extract per litre of water?

It depends on the grade and concentration. Follow the label, or ask us for the dose chart for the grade you buy.
